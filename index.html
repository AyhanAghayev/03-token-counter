What is an LLM?

LLM stands for Large Language Model. It is a type of artificial intelligence model trained on massive amounts of text data to understand and generate human language.

LLMs work by analyzing patterns in text and predicting the next token in a sequence. During training, the model learns grammar, facts, reasoning patterns, and language structure from books, websites, articles, and other datasets.

Today, LLMs are used for chatbots, coding assistants, translation, summarization, research, and many other tasks.

Examples of popular LLMs include GPT-4o by OpenAI, Claude 3.5 Sonnet by Anthropic, Gemini 1.5 Pro by Google, and Llama 3 by Meta.

What is a Token?

A token is a small chunk of text processed by a language model. Tokens are usually around 3–4 characters or about three quarters of a word on average.

LLMs do not read full sentences directly. Instead, they break text into tokens and process them one by one. Tokens are important because context windows and pricing are measured in tokens.

For example, the sentence "Hello, world!" could be split into tokens like:

"Hello" | "," | "world" | "!"

Different languages consume tokens differently. English is usually more token-efficient than Azerbaijani because Azerbaijani words often contain more suffixes and longer word forms.

For example:

English: "I am going to school."
Azerbaijani: "Mən məktəbə gedirəm."

The Azerbaijani sentence may use more tokens because of the richer word structure and suffix combinations.
